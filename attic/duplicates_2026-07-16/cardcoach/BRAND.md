## 01 · Brand Foundation

**What CardCoach is:** a Canada-only credit card rewards app. It helps Canadians with
multiple cards know which card to tap at checkout — based on real math, not guesswork.
Mobile-first, privacy-first, bilingual (English and French), built in Toronto.

**Core promise:** *You have the cards. We have the answer.* The recommendation is based on
actual value — not headline multipliers. The engine weighs base earn rates, category
bonuses, caps, point valuations, exclusions, conditions, and applicable promotions.

**What CardCoach is NOT:** a full personal-finance suite · a US product (Canada-first,
always) · a bank or issuer · a bank-login or scraping product · an "AI-powered" tool (never
use this phrase) · a speculative points-arbitrage engine.

**Market position:** Canada-first · simpler than hobbyist points spreadsheets · more
trustworthy than generic reward advice · more privacy-conscious than bank-login-heavy
products · more actionable than static comparison sites.

## 02 · Audience

**Primary — The Thoughtful Spender:** *"I picked these cards for a reason. I just can't
remember which reason applies right now."* Not a points nerd, not disengaged. Made smart
card choices once and wants them to keep paying off without a spreadsheet. Age 28–50, 2–5
cards, $75K–$180K household, urban/suburban Canadian. Emotional hook: a low hum of guilt
about not optimizing — CardCoach replaces it with ease.

**Secondary — The Quiet Optimizer:** *"I know the rules better than most. I want a tool
that respects that."* Points-savvy, reads the fine print, wants depth and reliability. Win
them with data quality and they become the loudest advocates.

---

## 03 · Personality

**Four pillars:** **Calm Confidence** (knowing, not shouting) · **Warm Intelligence**
(rigorous math, human delivery) · **Light-First** (users are in grocery stores and coffee
shops, not dim trading floors) · **Invisible Effort** (95 cards, 442 earn rates, 155 caps —
the user sees one answer).

**The Checkout Test** — the single most important rule in the system:
> *Would you say this to someone while they're holding up the checkout line?*

If no — too long, too formal, too vague, too alarming — rewrite it. Every piece of copy
passes this test. Every time.

## 04 · Logo System

**The Chip:** a single credit card, tilted and lifted, with a tangerine EMV chip in the
upper-left and a thin tangerine shelf line beneath suggesting where it was pulled from —
the moment of selection, frozen.

- Tangerine EMV chip — instantly signals "credit card" at any size
- 7-degree tilt — the card is being chosen, pulled from the stack
- Tangerine shelf line — the origin; suggests motion and selection
- Subtle drop shadow — hovering, not flat
- Clear space — 1× mark height on all sides, always
- Minimum size — 24px mark-only, 32px full lockup

**Wordmark:** "Card" in Poppins Bold (700) in Bark (#4A3F35); "Coach" in Poppins Regular
(400) in Tangerine (#E8734A). The weight contrast mirrors the product — your cards are
stable; the coaching is active and warm.

**Two primary variants:**

| Variant | Card color | Use on |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Primary (Light) | Indigo card, tangerine chip + shelf | Ivory, cream, butter, white, cool gray — most light surfaces |
| Dark | Ivory card, tangerine chip + shelf | Indigo, bark, black, dark slate — any dark surface |

*Edge case — brand-color surfaces:* on a tangerine/copper surface you don't control (partner
materials, event signage), flip the shelf and chip to ivory; the card stays indigo. Rare by
design, since tangerine backgrounds violate the 10% rule.

**Logo rules:** never flatten the tilt · never recolor the chip (tangerine is invariant) ·
never use angular corners (8px radius is intentional) · never put the logo on tangerine
unless unavoidable · the shelf line may drop below 20px (favicon) but is present everywhere
else · never add taglines, URLs, or text within the clear space.

## 05 · Color Palette

Every color has a specific job. Don't mix them up.

**Primary**

| Color | Hex / CSS var | Role |
|-------|---------------|------|
| Deep Indigo | `#2B3A67` / `--indigo` | Trust, headings, dark surfaces, card body |
| Tangerine | `#E8734A` / `--tangerine` | Action moments, CTAs, accent words. **MAX 10%** of any layout. |
| Sage | `#3CB58C` / `--sage` | Earned value, positive moments, success states |
| Ivory | `#FDF8F3` / `--ivory` | Primary canvas, light-mode background |

**Supporting**

| Color | Hex / CSS var | Role |
|-------|---------------|------|
| Cream | `#F5EDE4` / `--cream` | Card surfaces, secondary backgrounds, input fields |
| Buttery Yellow | `#F5D678` / `--butter` | Editorial highlights, featured callouts. **MAX 5%.** Never CTAs. |
| Bark | `#4A3F35` / `--bark` | Body text, all reading copy |
| Dusk | `#8B93A1` / `--dusk` | Metadata, secondary text, timestamps, muted labels |
| Copper | `#C4875C` / `--copper` | Pro tier, premium moments, achievements only |

**Color rules:** never use red as an action color (tangerine is warm; red is alarming) ·
tangerine is action-only, max 10% · buttery yellow is editorial-only, max 5%, never buttons
or text on ivory · light-first always (indigo is for small accent areas — footers, download
blocks, occasional section headers) · copper is Pro-tier only.

**Contrast reference:** Bark on Ivory 7.2:1 (AAA) · White on Indigo 10.4:1 (AAA) · Tangerine
on Ivory 3.1:1 (AA Large, 18px+ bold only) · Bark on Butter 7.9:1 (AAA) · Bark on Cream
6.4:1 (AAA).

## 06 · Typography

Three fonts, each with a clear role. No overlap.

| Font | When | Weights |
|------|------|---------|
| **Literata** | Hero headlines, editorial moments, italic accent words only | 700 |
| **Poppins** | Everything UI — body, subheads, buttons, captions, labels. Canva-compatible. | 400 / 500 / 600 / 700 |
| **IBM Plex Mono** | Metadata, step labels, timestamps, data, small caps | 400 / 500 |

**Italic rule:** Literata italic is used exclusively on hero headlines for accent words that
carry emotional weight, always rendered in tangerine. Poppins is NEVER italicized anywhere.

**Font loading (Google Fonts):**
```
https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=IBM+Plex+Mono:wght@400;500&family=Literata:ital,opsz,wght@0,7..72,700;1,7..72,700&family=Poppins:wght@400;500;600;700&display=swap
```

**CSS variable mapping:** `--ui` → `'Poppins', sans-serif` · `--hero` → `'Literata', serif`
· `--mono` → `'IBM Plex Mono', monospace`

> Note: Poppins is current (v1.1). The older `Outfit` UI font from v1.0 was superseded for
> Canva compatibility — do not flag Poppins as stale.

## 07 · Voice & Tone

CardCoach speaks like a helpful friend who happens to know every credit card in Canada. Not
performing expertise — delivering it.

**Five voice anchors:** **Warm** (friendly, human — not robotic) · **Certain** (confident,
clear — not hedging) · **Brief** (efficient — not cold/terse) · **Honest** (says "we don't
know" when true — not evasive) · **Encouraging** (celebrates wins — not patronizing).

**Voice in practice:**

| Context | Too corporate ✗ | Just right ✓ |
|---------|----------------|--------------|
| Recommendation | Based on our algorithmic analysis, we recommend Cobalt. | Cobalt is your best bet here — 5x on groceries. |
| When unsure | Data unavailable. Consult your issuer. | We haven't confirmed this card's FX fee yet, so we're leaving it out. |
| Cap warning | WARNING: Spending threshold approaching. | Your Cobalt grocery cap is almost full. After that, your TD takes over at 3%. |
| Hero headline | Optimize your credit card rewards. | You have the cards. We have the answer. |
| CTA button | Get Started Today! | Get the App |
| Email subject | Revolutionary Rewards Platform Launch! | The card you should have used at Metro. |

**Never use:** "Optimize" (say "best/right card") · "AI-powered" / "AI" in any user-facing
context · "Algorithmic" · "WARNING" · "Revolutionary / game-changing / disruptive" ·
exclamation marks · maple-leaf emoji.

## 08 · Implementation Rules

**Always:** lead with light mode (dark is secondary) · lead with the answer, then show the
work · 8px border radius on all cards, buttons, inputs · run the Checkout Test before
shipping copy · include EN and FR in architectural planning from day one · meet WCAG 2.1 AA
minimum · tangerine for CTAs exclusively.

**Never:** dark mode as primary expression · the word "optimize" in user-facing copy · red
for any action color · angular corners on the logo · analysis before the answer · describe
CardCoach as "AI-powered" · the word "WARNING" in UI copy · invent card facts, earn rates,
caps, exclusions, or point values · imply bank login or credential access · tangerine on
>10% of a layout · buttery yellow on CTAs or as text on ivory.

**Bilingual architecture:** `/en/` and `/fr/` URL prefixes · all copy externalized into
locale JSON (`en.json`, `fr.json`) · `hreflang` tags on every page (en-CA / fr-CA) · legal
pages need separate French legal copy (rewritten, not translated) · FAQ needs
human-reviewed French (never machine-only) · Quebec is a distinct market, not just a
translation.

**Accessibility baseline:** skip-to-content as first focusable element · semantic HTML (nav,
main, section, footer) · visible `focus-visible` states (2px tangerine outline) · no
information by color alone · descriptive alt text / aria-labels on all images and icons ·
support `prefers-reduced-motion`.
